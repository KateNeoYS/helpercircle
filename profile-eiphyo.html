The essentials

Currently

Six months with her present family — three children aged eleven, nine and six, the cooking, the cleaning and the family’s pets.

Nine years in Singapore

Continuously employed since January 2017, confirmed against her MOM record. Five employers, with one gap of about ten weeks in 2019. Every role ended as a transfer, none for cause.

Elderly care

Substantial, though not in her present home. She spent twenty-one months with an elderly couple before this role, five years in a household where she cared for the grandparents alongside a young child, and a year with a grandmother who had fallen and could not stand unaided.

Languages

Myanmar and fluent Chinese. Her English is weak, so a Chinese-speaking household would suit her best.

Cooking

Chinese home dishes — old cucumber soup, lotus root and pork rib soup, bak kut teh, steamed kampong chicken, mala vegetables, wok fried noodles and steamed fish. A little Thai, and happy to learn a family’s own menu.

With children

Three children of eleven, nine and six in her present home. In her five-year household she looked after a boy from kindergarten onward. Earlier still, three children of nine, six and four alongside an elderly grandmother. She takes them out on her own by public transport, and her employer says she plays with them off her own bat.

Where she needs direction

Her current employer is straightforward about this: Ei Phyo is not naturally the most organised, and does better on housework when a family sets out clearly how they like things kept. A previous employer of five years found her skilful once she knew the routine.

Marketing

Trusted with the weekly marketing — fish, vegetables, meat, poultry and household basics — going to the wet market on her own with little guidance.

Available from

Now, and she must transfer by 13 September

Age

33

Her employers’ recommendations

In their words, and ours.

Ei Phyo is being passed on for a reason that has nothing to do with her work. Her family are relocating to Malaysia, and she is not able to continue with them there. Ayumi is referring her to us herself.

She also holds a written reference from the family she worked for over five years before that, from 2019 to 2024. Two employers, five years apart, describing the same person.

Ayumi, her current employer, who is referring her to us:

“She is kind, honest and reliable. She is independent, she brings the kids out on her own, even taking public transport. She can cook pretty well.”

“She loves cooking and playing with kids. She takes initiative to play with kids.”

“Need more housework guidance as she is not very organised.”

Her previous employer of five years, writing in May 2024:

“Ei Phyo started her employment as a caregiver to my son when he was in kindergarten. Besides looking after my son’s diet, she also helps to pass on the desirable habits and discipline to him. She converses in simple Chinese for work purpose and able to pronounce some English words.”

“She is skilful in general household task daily cleaning, from handwashing socks to operating washing machine and vacuum machine. She is a fast learner in this area. As part of her work scope, she needs to dismantle the sofa using screwdriver to remove the cover for washing.”

“Ei Phyo is trusted in charge of weekly marketing of fish, vegetables, meat, poultry, toiletries and basic necessities. She travels to wet market herself with minimum guidance. She goes out on her own and returns back at a fixed timing.”

“Ei Phyo is honest, kind, pleasant and helpful personality. She is a devoted Buddhist who makes donations regularly during her country’s festivals. She is also an independent thoughtful worker who would strive to do her work better to try make her employer happy.”

Where she’d thrive next: a Chinese-speaking family with children, since her Chinese is fluent and her English is weak. Her two employers describe her the same way where the children and the marketing are concerned — independent, trusted to go out and come back. On the housework they differ: Ayumi would like her to be more organised, while the family she spent five years with found her skilful once she had learned their routine. A household that sets out how it likes things kept will get the best of her.

Her previous employer’s written reference is available to interested families, with her consent.
