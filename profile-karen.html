Her record in Singapore

Two families in seven years, and both contracts seen through to the end.

2019 to November 2022 — a Chinese family with two children of three and five. She showered them, fed them and helped with their homework, alongside the cleaning, the washing and the ironing. The contract ran its full course and was not renewed — the family wanted the children to become more independent.

November 2022 to November 2026 — the family she is with now: two sons, a dog, the cooking and the whole household. Four years, finishing on her contract’s end date. She is moving because she would like a different household, not because anything went wrong.

What her employer wrote. Kelly describes her as patient with the dog, positive, and willing to help; able to manage the general housework and to cook Chinese dishes, including for gatherings on her own days off. She is also specific about how Karen works: “She works best when there is a constant clear routine and guidance to the expected standards.”

Why we have kept that line. It is the most useful sentence in the letter. Karen is not someone who will arrive and reorganise your house; she is someone who will learn your way of doing things and keep to it for years. For a family with their own routines and no wish to hand them over, that is the arrangement they are after. For a family who want the house run without them thinking about it, it is not.

Where she’d suit next. A household that cooks Chinese and keeps a clear routine, ideally with a dog. Children of school age are fine; babies and eldercare are not what she has done.

Her employer’s signed reference, and her full employment history, are available to interested families — with her consent.
