The essentials

Available from

Now. Transfer within Singapore.

Nearly eight years, one family

November 2018 to September 2026 — a household of three, and the grandfather in her care. Seven years and ten months.

What that care involved

Advanced kidney disease and high blood pressure. Every morning she checked his blood pressure, blood sugar and weight, gave his medication and his meals, bathed him, changed him and moved him to his wheelchair.

Before that

Two years with a lady in her seventies — bathing, cooking and the household, and going out with her wherever she went. A physically demanding role she managed on her own.

Why she is available

The man she cared for passed away on 19 September 2026, after nearly eight years in her care. Nothing to do with the standard of her work.

Training

A caregiver course at the Golden Lotus Training Centre. Self-reported, and consistent with the work her employment record shows.

Languages

Burmese, with basic English and basic Chinese — enough for daily care and instructions, not for long conversation. Worth a short call to hear for yourself.

Best fit

A household caring for an elderly parent or grandparent, particularly one who is unwell, immobile or needs daily medical routines. Not a role centred on young children.

Her record in Singapore

Ten years, two households — and both of them eldercare.

October 2016 to September 2018 — a lady of seventy-five who weighed about a hundred and ten kilos. Lay Lay bathed her, cooked, kept the house, and went with her whenever she left it. Two years of work that was physical as much as anything else.

November 2018 to September 2026 — a household of three in a five-room flat, and their grandfather. He had high blood pressure and kidney disease that reached stage four. Her mornings began with his blood pressure, his blood sugar and his weight; then his medication, his meals, his bath, changing him, and lifting him into his wheelchair. She did that for seven years and ten months. He passed away on 19 September.

What her record says. Two households in ten years, both of them the care of one elderly person, each ending only when that person no longer needed care. Families do not keep someone for nearly eight years through an illness of that kind unless they trust her with it.

Where she’d suit next. A household with an elderly parent or grandparent, especially one who is unwell or no longer mobile. Asked what she does for pleasure, she wrote: taking care of elderly. It is the only kind of work she has done in Singapore.

Her employment history is available to interested families, with her consent.
