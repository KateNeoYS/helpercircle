Five years, one family

The household

A Western family of four — French and Swedish — in a private apartment. A stay-at-home father, a working mother, and two boys who are ten and almost thirteen, at the French International School.

Why she is moving on

The family are leaving Singapore. Her employer’s letter puts it plainly: that is the only reason she is going.

What she handles

The children, the grocery shopping, the cleaning, the laundry and the cooking — the whole running of the house.

Cooking

Western food, and she is very comfortable with it — a wide variety of meals for the family over five years.

Both parents travel abroad a couple of times a year and leave the boys with her. That is the sentence in her reference that does the most work.

How she works

Independently. Her employer writes that she understands the routines of a family home and works without needing supervision, managing the shopping and the day-to-day herself.

With children

Her employer’s recommendation

In their words, and ours.

Beverly has been with the same family for five years. She started with them in Hong Kong, and when the family relocated to Singapore in April 2022 they brought her along — which is its own kind of reference, before anyone writes anything down.

They are a Western family of four, French and Swedish: a stay-at-home father, a working mother, and two boys who were small when she started and are now ten and almost thirteen. Her work has been the children, the grocery shopping, the cleaning, the laundry and the cooking.

What her employer wrote: that Beverly is “trustworthy, dependable, hardworking and caring,” and that she “understands the routines of a family home and is able to work independently without needing supervision.”

And the line that matters most: she is someone they have been able to trust with their children “including when my husband and I have travelled abroad a couple times per year.” Leaving two boys and a household with someone for a week at a time, more than once a year, for five years, is not a thing families do lightly.

Why she is available: the family are leaving Singapore. Their letter is explicit that this is the only reason she is going, and they have offered to speak with any family who would like to hear about her work directly.

Where she’d suit next: a family with children who want someone to take the house off their hands as well — the shopping, the cooking, the laundry, without being asked twice. Western cooking is where she is most fluent. Childcare is what she has asked for most.
