Her Singapore household

The child in her care

Her record

One Singapore household, since February 2025.

February 2025 to now — nearly two years. A family of five in a five-room flat: her employers and their three children. The youngest is hers to look after — two years old when she arrived, three now — and the rest of the household comes with him: the cooking twice a day, the cleaning, the laundry, and the school morning and afternoon for an older child. Her employment record carries the whole stretch as one unbroken registration.

Her day, in her own words. She is up at six to get the older child ready and out, and cleans while the house is quiet. At half seven she wakes the little one — milk, a shower, and the drop at his school. The middle of the day is the house and the cooking; the older child is back at half two, and dinner is prepared after. She takes the little one to his appointments.

The cooking. Two meals a day, the evening one mostly Chinese and set by her ma’am — pork soup, pork in soy sauce, prawns in oyster sauce, chilli prawns, cuttlefish, chicken curry, porridge — with something simpler earlier: nasi goreng, fried vermicelli, bapao, nuggets. Daily family cooking, done to an instruction, rather than a menu of her own.

Her contract completes early in 2027. She has been offered a renewal and would rather move for a higher basic, which is the only reason she is looking.

Where she’d suit next. A family with a small child — a toddler above all, which is what she has had for the last two years — who want the child, the cooking and the house in the same pair of hands, and who are happy to say what they would like cooked. She has run a five-room flat and a three-year-old alongside a school run.

Her employment record and her full work history are available to interested families, with her consent.
