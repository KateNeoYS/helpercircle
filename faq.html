About Helper Circle

Helper Circle is a direct-choice hiring platform for families looking for domestic helpers with clearer trust signals and more context behind each profile.

You browse available helpers yourself, see why each one is listed, and choose whom you want to meet. Some are recommended by previous employers, some have strong employment records, and others have had their experience reviewed directly by Helper Circle.

If both sides decide to proceed, we support the employment paperwork through to completion.

We coordinate the introduction, help verify the information behind each profile, and support the employment paperwork if both sides decide to proceed.

You stay in control of the hiring decision throughout — we do not simply hand you a pre-selected shortlist.

A family who has lived with a helper knows things a profile cannot capture — how she is with a nervous toddler, whether she asks when she is unsure, what she is like on a difficult morning. When a previous employer recommends her, we confirm it with them directly and pass on that context, so you start the conversation already knowing something real.

Facebook helper groups can be useful, but they are unmoderated — anyone can post, and it is hard to know who you are speaking with. Every helper here carries at least one clear trust signal: a recommendation from an employer we have spoken to, several years with one family, a record of completed employment in Singapore, or experience Helper Circle has assessed directly. We review every profile before it goes live.

Introductions are also coordinated privately, so contact details are never publicly exposed.

“Every helper here carries a signal we can point to — a family’s recommendation, an employment record we’ve reviewed, or experience we’ve assessed directly. That is the entire point.”

Transfers & Hiring

A transfer is when a helper already working in Singapore moves from her current employer to a new employer without first returning home. The new employer applies for her Work Permit as a transfer, and once the new permit is issued, her existing Work Permit is automatically cancelled.

Yes. Transfers are a normal, MOM-recognised route, provided the Work Permit requirements are properly completed. We handle the employment paperwork and Work Permit process for you.

Yes. Most helpers listed here are currently in Singapore, but some may be overseas — including helpers between contracts or those applying from their home country. The introduction still starts with a video call so you can meet and decide before proceeding.

The paperwork differs. A new Work Permit is applied for rather than transferred, and depending on her home country there may be source-country or embassy requirements to meet as well. That usually makes the timeline longer than a local transfer. We guide and handle the employment and Work Permit process with you.

The most common reasons: the previous family is relocating overseas, their children have grown up and no longer need a helper, or an elderly family member they cared for has passed away or moved into a care facility.

Each profile includes the reason for transfer — reviewed with the referring employer where there is one.

Pricing & Fees

Yes. Browsing profiles, requesting an introduction and interviewing are all free. The S$699 fee is payable only once both sides agree to proceed.

Everything from your first enquiry through to completion of the employment process: your requirement review, suitable introductions, interview and feedback coordination, expectation alignment, the service agreement and employment documents, and the Work Permit or transfer process.

No fee is charged for browsing or interviewing. It is payable by the hiring employer only, once both sides agree to proceed. Helpers never pay Helper Circle — no placement fees, no salary deductions, no agency loans. Government and third-party charges are set by those providers and paid directly to them.

Government and third-party costs sit outside the S$699 fee. These include MOM Work Permit fees, insurance and any required medical examination. For non-Malaysian helpers, MOM also requires a S$5,000 security bond, normally arranged through a bank or insurer and often packaged with the insurance. We do not mark up these external costs.

These costs vary depending on permit requirements and insurance providers. Separating them keeps pricing transparent and avoids hidden markups.

The S$699 is a one-time service fee paid by the hiring employer after both sides agree to proceed. There is no separate placement fee, and nothing is charged for browsing or interviewing.

Verification & Safety

Every listed helper carries at least one clear trust signal:

Employer Recommended — a current or former employer recommends her, and Helper Circle confirms this with them.
Long-Term Employment — several years with one family, supported by employment records reviewed by Helper Circle.
Completed Contracts — a record of completed employment in Singapore, reviewed before listing.
Helper Circle Assessed — her work history and experience have been reviewed directly by Helper Circle before listing.

These signals come from different sources and are not meant to imply that every helper has been personally recommended by a previous employer.

Employer Recommended is our strongest signal: a family who employed her has vouched for her, and we have confirmed that with them directly. You get their words, not ours.

Long-Term Employment means she stayed with one family for several years, supported by employment records reviewed by Helper Circle. No one has personally recommended her — her record is the point.

Completed Contracts means she has completed employment in Singapore and we reviewed that record before listing her. It shows stability, not endorsement.

Helper Circle Assessed means we reviewed her work history and experience ourselves before listing her. There is no previous employer speaking for her.

Every profile shows exactly one badge, so you always know which of these you are looking at.

Before a profile goes live, we review the information and records relevant to the trust signal shown on her profile. Where there is a referring employer, we speak with them directly rather than relying only on a written reference. If we cannot sufficiently support the trust signal being shown, we do not publish that signal or list the profile on that basis.
