The essentials

Available from

November 2026. Transfer within Singapore.

Five years, one family

October 2021 to 2026 — Rana’s household, on one unbroken registration. They are the ones recommending her, and they will take a family’s call themselves.

Where she is now

A second Singapore household — a child of eleven, an elderly member of the family in their seventies, and pets. The cooking and the house alongside.

What she is looking for

A family who will give her a proper break in the day. In the household she is in now her day runs from the morning through to the evening without one. She is not looking for lighter work — she works hard, and would like a household that works with her.

The child she raised

She joined Rana’s household when their son was seven and left when he was twelve — five years of school runs, homework and afternoons, the whole of his primary-school years.

Children she will take

Any age, and she says so herself. She has had a boy from seven to twelve and a girl of eleven in the household she is in now.

Elderly care

Rana’s household included the boy’s grandparents, both in their seventies and in good health, and her household now includes an elderly member too. Everyday support rather than nursing care.

Household

Cooking, cleaning, laundry, ironing and the day-to-day organisation of a home. Her employer describes her as able to handle the running of the household independently.

Indian cooking

She picked up Indian cooking over her five years with Rana’s family, in her employer’s words, quite well. Theirs was a mixed Asian table with pasta some nights, and she cooks Chinese, Western and Indo-Chinese too — photographs below.

How she works

Honest and trustworthy, in her employer’s words. She does best when expectations are communicated clearly and the routine is reasonably consistent — give her the schedule and she gets on with it alone.

Best fit

A household with a settled rhythm that is clear about what it wants and then lets her get on with it — children of any age, pets and an elderly member at home are all fine with her, and a kitchen where Indian or mixed Asian cooking is welcome suits her best.

Nationality & age

Filipino, 35.

From her kitchen

Photographs Ann took of her own cooking — a roast, Indo-Chinese chilli chicken, prawns, noodles, steamed fish and greens. Five years in one household is long enough to learn a family’s taste.

Notes from her employer

From Rana’s signed letter of recommendation.

“I am happy to recommend Ann, who has worked with my family in Singapore for approximately five years.”

“During her time with us, Ann became familiar with the running of our household and was able to handle her responsibilities independently. She is experienced in general household work, including cooking, cleaning, laundry, ironing and the day-to-day organisation of a home. During her tenure with us she picked up Indian cooking quite well.”

“Ann is honest, trustworthy and can generally be relied upon. Over the years, she learned our preferences well and could generally be relied upon to complete her work without requiring supervision.”

“Ann does best when expectations are communicated clearly and there is a reasonably consistent routine. She likes to work independently once the schedule has been given to her.”

That last line is the most useful sentence in the letter, and we have left it exactly as written. It is not a criticism — it is a description of the kind of home she suits. A household with a settled rhythm, that says what it wants and then lets her get on with it, will get five good years out of her, as this one did.

“As she moves on to her next employment, I sincerely wish her well. I expect her to make a positive contribution to the household.”

Where she’d thrive next: a family that keeps a reasonably regular routine and can be clear about what it wants, and that will give her a proper break in the day. Children of any age, pets and an elderly member at home are all fine with her, and anyone glad of a cook who already knows Indian and mixed Asian food will be well served.

Rana’s letter, and her details for a reference call, are available to interested families, with Ann’s consent.
