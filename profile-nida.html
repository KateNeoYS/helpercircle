The essentials

Available from

By 25 October 2026.

Eleven years, one family

She joined Ms Gu’s household on 7 July 2015 and is there still — eleven years in one home, confirmed against her MOM employment record.

Why she’s moving on

The household no longer needs full-time help. It is the family’s circumstances that have changed, not her work — and they are passing her on with a written recommendation.

Dementia care, and the school run

The elderly member of the household lived with dementia, and Nida cared for her through it — while also getting the family’s two children to school and home again. She carried both at once, for years.

The two children, all the way through

She joined when they were around twelve and seven, and they are twenty-three and eighteen now. School runs, homework hours, meals and the daily looking-after — she saw both of them from school age to grown.

Cooking & household

Cooking and the general running of a home, recorded as her job scope throughout her eleven years with the family.

Eighteen years here

In Singapore since October 2008, across private flats and HDB homes, in households from one person to five.

Her nature

Dedicated, honest and hard-working, in her employer’s words. Trustworthy, responsible, and with a good attitude.

Adaptable

“She is fit for any work assigned to her and can adapt well to different tasks and environments” — her employer, who has watched her do it for eleven years.

Best fit

A household carrying both ends at once — an elderly parent who needs real care, and children who need getting to school. She has done exactly that, in one home, for years.

Notes from her employer

From Ms Gu’s signed letter, dated 3 September 2026.

Eleven years is the whole of this page, really. Nida joined Ms Gu’s family in July 2015 and has been there ever since — caring for an elderly member of the household who lived with dementia, and at the same time getting two children to school and back, year after year, until they were grown.

“She can follow instructions and is able to handle general house works as well as taking care of elderly and taking care of two children.”

“I highly recommend Ms Nida for her dedication, honesty, and hard work. She is trustworthy, responsible, and has a good attitude. She has been an invaluable help to our family, and any future employer will benefit from her skills and commitment.”

“She is fit for any work assigned to her and can adapt well to different tasks and environments. I am confident that she will be able to meet the expectations of her future employer.”

Before this family she had already been in Singapore seven years, with a household she stayed with for three and a half. Two short placements in the winter of 2014 and 2015 sit between that one and this — and then eleven years without a move.

Where she’d thrive next: a household with a parent who needs care and children who need looking after — the two jobs at once that so many Singapore families are trying to cover. She has done it for eleven years, and when a household suits her she stays.

Ms Gu’s signed letter is available to interested families, with Nida’s consent.
