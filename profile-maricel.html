The essentials

Available from

1 December 2026, when her contract ends. Transfer within Singapore.

Her household now

Three years and ten months, since December 2022 — a household of four in a private flat, with the children, the cooking, the house and a family pet. Her contract was renewed along the way.

Four and a half years before that

Four years and six months, December 2011 to June 2016 — a household of five in a landed home. Her first job in Singapore.

Children she has cared for

Newborn through to school age. Her current household covers infant, toddler and child; an earlier family had two daughters of eleven and thirteen, with the breakfasts, the uniforms and the after-school runs.

Cooking

Filipino, Chinese and Western — satay, pancit, braises, soups, stir-fried greens, a steak and vegetables. She cooked in a food court in the Philippines before coming to Singapore. Photographs below.

Pets

Yes, and willingly — a dog in an earlier household, walked and fed daily, and a pet in her home now.

Why she is leaving

Her own decision. She will see her contract through to its end date and has chosen not to recontract — after nearly four years she would like to try a different household.

Eldercare

None, and she says so herself. Her work has been children, the cooking and the household.

Her record

Fifteen years in Singapore, and three families who kept her.

December 2011 to June 2016 — four years and six months. Her first job in Singapore, a household of five in a landed home.

June 2016 to February 2018 — one year and eight months. An Italian family in Bukit Timah with two daughters of eleven and thirteen and a dog. The breakfasts, the school uniforms, the playdates and activities, the dog walked and fed, and all of the cooking. It ended when the family left Singapore, and it is this employer who wrote the reference quoted at the top of this page.

February 2018 to December 2019 — three households, of five months, one year and two months, and three months. This is the one unsettled stretch in fifteen years. She will talk it through with you plainly.

December 2019 to February 2022 — two years and two months. A family in a five-room flat, their child, the cooking and the household.

February 2022 to December 2022 — nine months. A household of three in a landed home, with an infant. It ended when the family moved back to Europe.

December 2022 to now — three years and ten months. A household of four in a private flat: a child from infancy, the cooking, the house and the family pet. Her employment record carries this as one unbroken registration, which is what a renewed contract looks like. She is leaving because she has chosen not to recontract, not because anything went wrong.

About the reference. It is a two-page letter written in January 2018, when that family left Singapore. It sets out what she handled in their home — the girls’ mornings, the school runs, the dog, Filipino, Chinese, Tex-Mex, American and Italian cooking — and says she worked without supervision throughout. It is a genuine and warm reference, and it is also eight years old: it describes her as she was between 2016 and 2018, not as her current family would describe her today.

Where she’d suit next. A family with young children — she has taken a child from infancy twice over — who want the cooking in the same pair of hands. She is used to working without being told what to do next, which suits a household where both parents are out. She has not done eldercare.

Her full employment history and the reference letter itself are available to interested families, with her consent.
