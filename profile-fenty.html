The essentials

Available from

Mid-January 2027. Transfer within Singapore.

Where she is now

Since June 2023 — a family of five from China with three children: twins of nine, a boy and a girl, and a child of ten. All three are at school.

The school run

Out of the door at seven for the morning drop-off and back at three for the pick-up, with dinner cooked in between on the days it has to be. She does all of it for three children.

Weekends

Saturday swimming at seven for the boy, ballet for the girl, then all three to tuition — and home again by bus. The house and the cooking fit around it.

Why she is available

She is ready for a change of environment after three and a half years in the same house. Nothing in her record or her account suggests anything else.

Three years seven months, one family

Her Singapore record is short and almost entirely one household — a single unbroken registration from June 2023 to now, through a contract renewed rather than restarted.

Before Singapore

Three years in Taiwan, 2006 to 2009, caring for an elderly gentleman in a household with his wife and their grown son. He walked slowly, and took a wheelchair for anything far. That work is from her own account — a Singapore employment record does not cover work done abroad.

Elderly care

She is willing, and has done it before. Dementia is where she draws the line, and she says so upfront rather than finding out later.

Languages

Fluent Chinese, which is the language of the household she is in now. Her English is basic — worth knowing if nobody at home speaks Mandarin.

Cooking and pets

Chinese and Western, cooked around the school run. She has no photographs of her cooking to hand. No pets — she would rather not have animals in the house, and says so plainly.

A week, in her own words

How the days run in the household she is in now. Her English, lightly punctuated.

“In the morning I took the children to school at 7 am. And picking him up at 3 o’clock. I was on the way — before I went to pick him up I cooked dinner for mam and sir and the children.”

“Tuesday and Thursday are the schedule for cooking dinner before picking up the children. I take my children’s dinner to school because I arrive there at 8 pm.”

“Saturday I took him to swimming lessons for boys at 7 in the morning. And ballet lessons for girls during the day. So that Saturday I took 3 children to go to tutoring, and after noon I sent my tutoring girls home by bus with the 2 boys. Then I cleaned the house and cooked, and in the afternoon I picked up my daughter by bus.”

Her record

In Singapore since May 2023, and in Taiwan before that.

2006 to 2009 — three years in Taiwan. An elderly gentleman in her care, day to day, in a household with his wife and their grown son. He walked on his own but slowly, and went by wheelchair for anything far. This is her own account rather than anything a Singapore record can confirm, and she is happy to talk it through.

2009 to 2023. At home in Indonesia, with her own family, before returning to work abroad.

May to June 2023. Her first Singapore household, seven weeks — a mother from China with children of three and five, in a five-room flat. She transferred.

June 2023 to now — three and a half years. A family from China: the parents, twins of nine and a child of ten, all three at school. The cooking, the general household, the school run twice a day and the Saturday lessons. One unbroken registration, a contract completed and renewed, and she is leaving only for a change of environment.

Where she’d suit next. A Mandarin-speaking household with children — any age, she says, and she has run a week around three of them at once. An elderly parent at home is no obstacle, short of dementia. Not a home with pets, and not one where nobody speaks Mandarin.

Her employment record and her full work history are available to interested families, with her consent.
