The essentials

Currently

Five months with a Singaporean Malay family, caring for their six-year-old daughter. She gets her ready for school and walks her there and back, and handles the cooking and general cleaning.

Why she is moving on

The family have secured a place for their daughter in an after-school care programme, and no longer need the same help. Their letter says they are parting ways with her solely for that reason.

Before that

A three-generation Chinese household in a three-storey home, four bedrooms and four bathrooms, with two independent children of ten and twelve. She did the cleaning, the cooking and the gardening.

Earlier

A Singaporean family with children of twelve and sixteen. That role ended when the father moved the family’s business to the United States.

Children

School-age rather than infants. Her three households have had children of six, ten, twelve and sixteen, and her current employer describes the school run as her daily work.

Cooking

Everyday family cooking. Her employer notes that she cooks capably and will follow a recipe found online, so a family with their own dishes could teach her theirs.

Her employer’s recommendation

In her employer’s words, and ours.

Dewi has spent the past five months with a family in Singapore, caring for their young daughter. Her employer wrote us a reference for her in August, and was clear that the parting has nothing to do with how Dewi has worked.

In her employer’s own words:

“Dewi’s primary responsibility was caring for our child. Her daily job scope includes prepping our child for school, as well as both sending off and picking up from school. She is also responsible for general house cleaning and cooking.”

“Dewi is capable of cooking and also following recipe online.”

“In term of character, Dewi is dedicated and always put in effort into task she is told to do.”

“We are parting ways with Dewi solely because we have secured a placement for our daughter in after school care program which we feel will benefit our daughter more from an academic environment as our child is growing older.”

Where she’d thrive next: a family who are happy to show her how they like things done, and who will say what they need. Her employer’s description is of someone dependable and willing who works well to direction, rather than someone who sets her own agenda. In a household with clear routines, that is a real strength.

Her employer’s full reference letter is available to interested families, with her consent.
