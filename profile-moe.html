The essentials

Available from

She is in Vietnam and can fly at any time, from Da Nang. This is a hire from overseas on a new work permit, not a transfer — set out below.

Five and a half years, one household

December 2020 to July 2026 — a man of around fifty-six who had had a stroke and could not walk. By the end it was the two of them in the flat.

What that care involved

Showering, dressing and toileting; wheelchair and bed transfers; diaper changing and urinary catheter care; medication and vital signs. He was around sixty kilos.

Before that

September 2018 to October 2019 — a lady of about eighty with dementia, who could still walk but needed help showering and moving. A household of four.

Training

A three-month Nurse Aid Training certificate, and two years as a volunteer nurse assistant at a township hospital in Myanmar — patient hygiene, feeding, mobility and vital signs.

Cooking & languages

Simple Singaporean and Chinese dishes, and soft or restricted meals for someone elderly. Burmese natively, English at a conversational level. No Chinese or dialect — she is learning, and says so plainly.

Her record

Two Singapore households since 2018, and nearly all of it eldercare.

2016 to 2018 — Myanmar. Two years as a volunteer nurse assistant at a township hospital, helping the nurses with basic patient care: hygiene, feeding, mobility and watching vital signs. She took a three-month nurse aide course alongside it.

September 2018 to October 2019. Her first Singapore household — a lady of about eighty with dementia, living with her son and daughter-in-law. She could still walk, but needed help showering and getting about, and Moe cooked for the family as well as preparing softer meals for her. The job ended when the elderly lady passed away.

February 2020 to July 2026. One household, though her employment record shows it as two registrations: the work permit was first held by the mother of the family, and moved to her daughter after the mother died. The second of those registrations runs unbroken for five years and seven months.

The person in her care was one of the sons — a man of around fifty-six who had had a stroke and could not walk. Showering, dressing, toileting, transfers in and out of the wheelchair and the bed, catheter care, his medication and his meals. The household thinned over the years until it was the two of them in the flat. It ended in July 2026 when he passed away.

Where she’d suit next. A household with an elderly or dependent adult who needs real physical care — someone no longer mobile, or recovering from a stroke. She has done five and a half years of exactly that, has the nurse aide training behind it, and is used to being the only pair of hands in the house. Her English is conversational rather than fluent and she speaks no Chinese, so a household that can give instructions in English would suit her best.

Her employment record, her certificate and her referee’s details are available to interested families, with her consent.

How a family would hire her

A hire from overseas — not a transfer

Moe is in Vietnam and can fly to Singapore at any time. A family hiring her would apply for a new work permit and bring her in, rather than taking over an existing one.

That means a new permit application, a medical examination, the security bond, insurance and an air ticket, and some weeks between agreeing and her starting.
