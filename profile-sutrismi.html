The essentials

Available from

By 29 November 2026. Transfer within Singapore.

Why she is available

The household she joined in September has not turned out to be the right fit. She said so early rather than carry on, and is looking for her next family.

Four years seven months, one household

February 2022 to September 2026 — an Indian Indonesian family of five: two young boys and a grandfather in good health, with the house and the cooking hers. It ended when the family moved back to Indonesia.

Caregiver training

Caregiver Basic Level 1, completed November 2020, and Caregiver Level 2, completed June 2021 — both at the Indonesian Embassy’s training centre in Singapore.

Dementia care

Two households, back to back. Two years living with one elderly woman whose dementia was in its earlier stages, until she died of a heart attack; then three months with another who needed a great deal more. Hospital trips, medicines, showering and the whole day.

Children she has cared for

Two boys of three and five in her four-year household. Before that, four children in one home — a newborn and children of two, five and seven — and an infant in a household in 2022.

Cooking and languages

Indonesian, Malay and Chinese food, and she works from a recipe when a family has one. English and Malay. Photographs further down this page.

Best fit

A household with an elderly person, dementia included, or young children with the house to run — she has done four and a half years of exactly that. Comfortable working alongside another helper or on her own.

Notes from her employer

From Sasivanen’s signed letter, written in June 2026 as his family prepared to leave Singapore.

He sets out the job: “She managed our household independently, including cleaning, laundry, grocery shopping, cooking, and caring for our two boys. She is trustworthy, hardworking, responsible, and always willing to learn.”

On how they worked together: “In our home, I gave her the flexibility to manage daily tasks in the way she felt worked best, which allowed her to demonstrate strong initiative and independence. Every household has different routines and expectations, and I believe she is respectful, adaptable, and willing to adjust to her employer’s preferences.”

And at the end: “This recommendation reflects my genuine experience working with her over the past four years. She has been a dependable support to our family, making our daily life much easier and giving us peace of mind. We are truly grateful for her dedication and contribution to our home during our time in Singapore. I highly recommend her to any family looking for a reliable, capable, and caring helper.”

Where she’d suit next

A household with an elderly person, or young children with the house to run — the two things her nine years here have been made of. She has two caregiving certificates behind the first and four and a half years of the second. She cooks Indonesian, Malay and Chinese food, she has had a newborn and small children before, and she speaks English and Malay.

Her full employment record, her written reference and her caregiving certificates are available to interested families, with her consent.
