Why she’s moving on

Her present family cook Indian food daily, and after years in European and Singaporean kitchens that style has not come naturally to her. It is a question of cuisine, not of care, and they are parting on good terms.

She starts where it is hardest

Every household she has joined has begun with a baby — twin girls from birth, a Dutch family’s newborn, a four-month-old, and an eighteen-month-old boy. That is an unusually consistent record.

Four years with twins

She joined Léna’s family when their twin girls were newborns and stayed four years, through the whole of the early stage. The girls, their mother writes, adore her still.

Routines

“She always showed up on time, managed the children’s routines with ease.” Her employer describes her as very well organised, and as a calm presence in a busy house.

Cooking

She cooked home meals for Léna’s family through their busiest weeks, and is at ease in European and Singaporean home cooking. Indian cuisine is not her strength.

Best fit

A family with a baby on the way or a very young child, who want someone who has done the newborn stage properly and more than once.

Twelve years here

In Singapore since June 2014, across five households — French, Dutch, two Singaporean families, and her present Indian and British family. Every one recorded her job scope as infant or toddler care. Confirmed against official MOM employment records.

Notes from her employer

From Léna’s letter, written in Singapore on 25 August 2025.

“We were lucky to have Menchie with our family for the past four years, caring for our twin girls from the time they were newborns. She has given them love, patience, and attention, and truly became part of our family.”

Newborn twins are the hardest version of the job, and she did it for four years to the day, from their first weeks until they were four.

“She always showed up on time, managed the children’s routines with ease, and helped us during our busy weeks by preparing delicious home-cooked meals. Her calm and caring presence brought much comfort to our home, and she supported us through the most important early years of parenting twins.”

What stands out across her whole record is how often she has been trusted with a child at the very beginning. Twin girls from birth, a Dutch family’s newborn, a four-month-old, an eighteen-month-old. Families who have been through that stage tend to keep the person who got them through it.

“We will always be grateful for her kindness and dedication. Any family would be lucky to welcome Menchie into their lives.”

Where she’d thrive next: a family expecting a baby, or with a very small one already, who would value calm hands and a person who keeps the routine steady when no one has slept.

Léna’s letter is available to interested families, and she is happy to be contacted, with Menchie’s consent.
