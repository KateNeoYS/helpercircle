The essentials

Available from

Available now. Transfer within Singapore, direct from the family she is with.

Why she is available

In her employer’s words, the family’s own caregiving needs have changed — the grandfather now needs more advanced care than the household was set up for. They are passing her on themselves.

Nearly eighteen years, one household

June 2002 to April 2020 — a grandmother of eighty-five whom she cared for to the end, and then the household itself for the years after that. One unbroken registration.

Four years of heavy care

2021 to 2025 — a woman of eighty-six who could not walk and had to be lifted and moved by wheelchair. The showering, the medicines, the whole day. It ended when she passed away.

Where she is now

Since June 2025 — a household of four, with an elderly person of seventy-plus who still walks: the nappy changes, the massage, the cleaning, the ironing and the cooking.

Children

She has not worked with children in ten years, and asks for a household of adults or an elderly person. Her employer says the same in his own note. Worth taking at face value.

Cooking

Malay, Punjabi, Chinese and Western. Her employer lists Chinese, Indian and Indonesian dishes, alongside the housework and washing the car.

Best fit

A household with an elderly person who needs hands-on care, where the house is hers to run. She has worked alongside a second helper and on her own, and is comfortable either way.

Notes from her employer

From the note Sherwin wrote when he began looking for her next household, November 2026.

He opens with the reason: “She has been reliable and with our family for over 1 year. However, our personal needs have changed due to advanced caregiving needs for Ah Kong.”

On the work: “She is able to cook Chinese, Indian and Indonesian dishes. Her responsibilities included house work, basic elder care, cooking and car washing.”

And on how she works: “She has 15+ years experience in Singapore and was able to independently manage the house — three bedrooms, two bathrooms. She is trustworthy.”

He is also plain about what she is not for: “She has little to no experience working with children in the last 10 years and prefers a household with adults or elderly.”

Her record

In Singapore since November 2001.

November 2001 to June 2002. Two short placements at the start of her working life, each caring for an elderly person, and each ending when that person passed away.

June 2002 to April 2020 — seventeen years and ten months. She joined at twenty-eight to look after a grandmother of eighty-five, in good health at first. After six or seven years the grandmother had a stroke, recovered, grew frail and died of old age, and Sukini stayed on to run the house. Her employer died some years later, at sixty-seven. She left in April 2020, when the pandemic changed the household. One registration, start to finish.

April to June 2020. Two months. The family that took her on found they could not meet her salary, and she moved on.

June to November 2020. Five months with a Malay family — the parents, a child of six for the school run, and the father’s mother. It was not a fit, and she asked to transfer.

June 2021 to June 2025 — four years. A landed house: a woman of eighty-six who could not walk, her daughter and son-in-law, and the daughter’s own mother in good health. Sukini had the showering, the medicines and the lifting, with a second helper for the cleaning. Her record carries the care of an elderly person, a person with special needs, and pets. It ended when the grandmother passed away.

June 2025 to now. A household of four in a condominium — the cleaning, the ironing, the cooking and the car, and an elderly person who still walks but needs changing and massage.

Where she’d suit next. A household with an elderly person. That is what she has done for most of twenty-five years, through every stage of it — a healthy grandmother who grew frail, a woman who could not walk at all, and an older person who still gets about. She cooks across four cuisines and runs a house without being told. Not a household with young children.

Her employment record and her full work history are available to interested families, with her consent.
