Available from

20 October 2026. Transfer within Singapore.

Four and a half years, one family

March 2022 to now — two adults and a child in a private flat, with the cooking and the household. Her contract was renewed more than once.

The child she has cared for

The same child from five months old to five — the whole of that span, in one home. Her employer writes that she was “kind and caring towards our child”.

Cooking

Filipino and Indian food, some Chinese, vegetarian dishes, and some Western. She follows recipes and would like to learn more Western cooking. Photographs below.

How she works best

Her employer puts it plainly: she “requires clear instructions and benefits from periodic reminders and reinforcement.” A household that gives direction will get the best of her; one that expects her to run itself will not.

Why she is leaving

The contract has run its course and the family no longer need a helper. They have written her a reference on her way out.

Pets and eldercare

No experience with pets, though she is willing to try. She would rather not take on eldercare, and says so herself.

Nine years in Singapore

Since September 2017, across four households — two of nearly two years and over four years, and one of a month. Both her previous and current employers are Indian expatriate families.

Her record

Nine years in Singapore, and a child she has had since he was five months old.

September 2017 to August 2019 — one year and eleven months. A household of five, her first job in Singapore.

August to September 2019 — one month, and then a short break before her next job.

January 2020 to February 2022 — two years and one month. A household of three. This placement ended at her own request.

March 2022 to now — four years and seven months. Two adults and a child in a private flat: the cooking, the household, and their child from five months old until five. Her employment record carries this as one unbroken registration, which is what a renewed contract looks like. She is leaving because the contract has ended and the family no longer need a helper.

The reference, in full. Divya P. wrote on 1 October 2026 that Sunshine “maintained a clean house”, was “kind and caring towards our child”, “can prepare basic Indian meals and can follow recipes on YouTube”, and is “friendly and ensured that everything met our family’s specific preferences”. She also wrote that Sunshine “requires clear instructions and benefits from periodic reminders and reinforcement”. We have included that sentence because a family deserves the whole letter, not the flattering half of it — and because it is useful. It tells you what kind of household she belongs in.

Where she’d suit next. A family with a young child who want Indian or Filipino food cooked at home, and who are comfortable setting out what they want rather than leaving it to her. She has not worked with pets, though she is willing to learn, and she would rather not take on eldercare.

Her full employment history and the reference letter itself are available to interested families, with her consent.
