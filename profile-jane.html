Seven years, one family

She joined her present Singapore family in March 2019, when their son was two months old. He is seven now. Cooking, cleaning, marketing, laundry, and him.

She has only ever done childcare

Every placement of her career has had a child at the centre of it — in four countries, over nearly twenty years. There is no part of her history that is housework alone.

Newborns, three times over

Newborn twins in Taiwan alongside a three-year-old, a newborn in Singapore in 2015, and her present boy from two months old. The first weeks are familiar ground.

Four countries

Taiwan, China, Turkey and Singapore — expat and local households, and languages and kitchens she had to learn from scratch each time.

Cooking

Taiwanese, Chinese, Turkish, Japanese — sushi and miso — Vietnamese wraps, Western, Filipino, and cheese platters. She follows new recipes from YouTube and is happy to keep learning.

The day around a child

School drop-offs and pickups, playdates when he was small, and running him to his activities most days. Marketing and the household besides.

Her Singapore record

Five placements since December 2015, supported by employment records we reviewed. One of them is the current seven and a half years. Two are short — ten weeks in early 2017 and three months over the winter of 2018–19 — and both sit either side of her two years in Istanbul. Her years in Taiwan, China and Turkey are her own account.

A family with a baby coming or a small child already, who would value someone who has done the newborn stage three times and can cook almost anything. She is equally happy with older children — asked what she would like next, she said newborns and older children both.

In her own words

Her account of nearly twenty years, four countries, and a great many children.

Taiwan, 2007–2012. Newborn twins and a three-year-old, for five years. The children, the house, the laundry, and dinner when the family were not eating out. She learned to cook Taiwanese there and still does.

China, 2014–2015. Two small children, aged two and four. Childcare and their clothes; the household was not hers.

Singapore, 2015–2016. A newborn and a three-year-old, with the cooking, cleaning and school run besides. One year only, because the family emigrated to Australia.

Istanbul, 2017–2018. A three-year-old, and the whole of a household — cleaning, ironing, marketing, cooking, and the school run. Turkish food joined her repertoire.

Singapore, 2019 to now. A boy from two months old to seven. Playdates when he was little, and now she picks him up most days and takes him on to whatever he is doing next.

Read together, it is one long specialism rather than a series of jobs. Somebody else’s baby, in an unfamiliar country, with a kitchen she had to learn — four times over.

Where she’d thrive next: a family expecting a child, or with a small one, who want somebody who has done the hardest part before and will cook them almost anything while she is at it.
