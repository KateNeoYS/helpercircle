Her record

Fourteen years as a helper, nine and a half of them with one Singapore family.

Four years in Bahrain. Her first work abroad, before Singapore. She did a good deal of the cooking there, and it is where her Western dishes come from.

August 2016 to February 2026 — nine years and six months. One family, one flat, one boy — hers to look after from the age of one until he was eleven. The cooking, the house, the school run, and as he grew, his English and his Tagalog and his homework. Her employer calls her a second mother to him; the two still see each other on her day off. The job ended in February because the family’s own pass status changed and they could no longer employ a helper — and that same family is the one recommending her now.

February to June 2026 — about three months. A Singaporean Chinese household with children of eight and eighteen, and a grandmother who came over often. She left because she was not given enough food. Each time she asked whether she could cook a little more, she was told there was already plenty. That is her account, and she is willing to talk it through with a family on a call.

June 2026 to now. A Filipino household whose children are already adults, where most of the work is the dog and the house. It is a family who have not had a helper before, and the day is not planned out loud: she is often not told in advance whether anyone will be eating at home, so she cooks in case, and then the food is not needed. Her former employer, who referred her, puts it simply — they are not used to having a helper, and it has not worked out.

Where she’d suit next. A family with school-age children who want the cooking and the house in the same pair of hands, and who would rather keep someone for years than change often. She does her best work where the day is set out clearly — told what is wanted and left to get on with it, which is how the nine and a half years worked. At 52 she is still strong and takes the housework in her stride; a baby under two is not the best use of what she is good at.

Her employment history and her referee’s contact details are available to interested families, with her consent.
