Her work in Singapore

Leny has been in Singapore since December 2019, across three households. Her account of each one matches her MOM record closely.

  • 2021 to 2025 · Turkish-Dutch family of four, four years. A landed home, two children. Cooking, meal planning, grocery shopping, the household, ironing and laundry, plus the school run. It ended only because the family left Singapore.
  • 2019 to 2021 · Chinese family of five, two years. Three children of eight, fourteen and sixteen, and the general household. She completed that contract too.
  • December 2025 to now · family of four. Two children of eight and ten, and the household. The placement has not turned out to suit either side. Her employer asked whether she would be happier with another family, and they agreed together that she would.

Family she may suit

  • A family with school-age children rather than babies. That is what she has done in all three of her households.
  • An expatriate family. Two of her three Singapore households were Dutch.
  • A home where the cooking matters and the family are happy for her to learn their dishes.
  • A household with settled routines. Her four-year family gave her a free hand in the kitchen and a clear weekly rhythm, and that is where she did her best work.

Her employer’s recommendation

In her employer’s words, and ours.

Leny spent four years with a Turkish-Dutch family and their two children. The only reason it ended was that the family left Singapore. They wrote this letter for her in June 2026.

In her employer’s own words:

“During these four years, Leny became much more than a helper to our family. She became a trusted part of our home and, over time, a valued member of our family.”

“One of Leny’s greatest talents is her cooking. Over the years, she prepared delicious Turkish, Western, Indonesian, Dutch, and Indian dishes for our family. She was always eager to learn new recipes and took pride in preparing meals that brought everyone together around the table.”

“We trusted her completely to take the children out independently, whether it was to the park, the playground, activities, or other outings. She would play games with them, keep them entertained, and actively engage with them rather than simply supervise them.”

“She was also responsible for bringing the children to school and picking them up. She was always punctual, reliable, and organized. Knowing that our children were in her care gave us complete peace of mind.”

“She approaches every task with a positive attitude and takes initiative without needing constant supervision. We always felt confident leaving our home and children in her care.”

“Even today, they speak about her with great affection and miss her dearly.”

Where she’d thrive next: in her employer’s words, “she deserves a home where she is heard, valued, and treated as an important member of the household.” A family with children old enough to be taken out, who would use the range she has in the kitchen.

Her employer’s full letter is available to interested families, and they have offered to answer questions directly.
