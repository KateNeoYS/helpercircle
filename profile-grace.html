Her employers’ recommendations

Three letters, from three families.

Grace has been in Singapore since September 2009 — seventeen years — and the first ten and a half of them were with one family in one flat. Three of the families she has worked for have put a reference in writing for her, which is more than most profiles here can show.

Florilynn, eleven years: two children taken through bath time, meals, play and bedtime, the school run, the cooking, the laundry and the wet market. “She has been a caring auntie to our kids, always ensuring their safety, cleanliness, and making sure they eat healthy food. She teaches them good manners, and the kids love her. The kids still talk to her up to now as she is considered part of the family.” Her closing line is the one to hold onto: “she is a helper worth keeping.”

Oxana B., three years: a different kind of praise — “a remarkable blend of calmness and wisdom”, children who developed a genuine affection for her, and one specific, unglamorous skill: she managed the household grocery budget herself, meticulously. Being trusted with money is not a small thing.

Dharshi H., 2023: a family of four with daughters of seven and three. Cooking, housekeeping, the school pick-up, snacks for the children. “Grace is quiet, friendly, can speak English well and is a fast learner. She is warm with the kids.” They add that she was seeking a home with greater remuneration, and close: “She is easy to be around so we hope she can find a family who will value her.”

Newborns are the part to notice. There is a baby in her present household and infant care sits on her permit scope, and across seventeen years she has taken children from their first weeks through to their teens. Families hiring for a newborn usually have to choose between someone who has done it and someone who will stay — she is both.

Why she is available: her present family are relocating, and her permit runs to 14 October.

Where she’d suit next: a family with children who want someone who has done this for seventeen years and would like to stay. What she asks for in return is modest and worth quoting — a kind and respectful family, clear communication, and a long-term home.

Two of her referees have offered to answer questions from interested families directly.
